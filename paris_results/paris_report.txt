Pick following Paris et al.: m3 M2 n2.
  r80 rule (most polymorphic r80 loci): M1n1 (12,314)
  Fig. 4 (later steps add <1.0% new polymorphic r80 loci): M1n1
  Polymorphic loci within individuals (heterozygous loci per individual) first
      level off: M2n2
  How: The r80 maximum (M1n1) lies below where polymorphic loci within
      individuals first level off (M2n2). None of Paris et al.'s datasets did
      this; in all three they chose M at or above where polymorphic loci
      within individuals levelled off (trout M5, earthworm M3, penguin M2), so
      the pick is M2n2. This applies their judgement to a case they did not
      meet; it is not a rule they state. Taken alone, their r80 rule gives
      M1n1.

Notes:
  - Polymorphic r80 loci fall at every step, from 12,314 at M1n1 to 10,193 at
    M6n6, so no step adds new polymorphic r80 loci (Paris Fig. 4). None of
    Paris et al.'s datasets did this: polymorphic r80 loci were highest at M2
    (penguin) and M5 (trout), and still rising at M11 (earthworm).
  - Polymorphic loci within individuals: Paris et al. chose M5 for trout,
    where these 'began to flatten out', and M3 for earthworm, where they
    'began to plateau'. Here they are heterozygous loci per individual,
    counted in each sample's ustacks output (before the catalog). They change
    by +66 ± 33* (37 of 44 up) at M1n1→M2n2, +8 ± 20 (22 of 44 up) at
    M2n2→M3n3, +60 ± 18* (37 of 44 up) at M3n3→M4n4, +93 ± 12* (44 of 44 up)
    at M4n4→M5n5 and +114 ± 20* (44 of 44 up) at M5n5→M6n6 (± 2 SE, paired
    over 44 individuals). They first level off (the next step does not rise
    beyond two standard errors) after M2n2.
  - Allele collapse within individuals (Paris Table S6a, 'the proportion of
    putative alleles that collapsed'): the share of each individual's stacks
    that ustacks merged into loci is 13.3% at M1n1, 23.8% at M2n2, 27.7% at
    M3n3, 29.2% at M4n4, 29.9% at M5n5 and 30.2% at M6n6. Of the change across
    the tested range, 62% has happened by M2n2, 85% by M3n3, 94% by M4n4 and
    98% by M5n5. Paris et al. saw this level out between M3 and M4 (trout), M1
    and M2 (penguin) and M2 and M3 (earthworm), and chose M5, M2 and M3.
  - From M1n1 to M2n2, each individual loses 5,013 loci on average but gains
    only 66 heterozygous loci, so most of the stacks merged at that step do
    not form a heterozygous locus. Raising M is removing loci rather than
    turning monomorphic loci polymorphic, which is how polymorphic r80 loci
    can fall while polymorphism within individuals still rises.
  - Above M2n2, heterozygous loci per individual rise again (+60 ± 18* at
    M3n3→M4n4, +93 ± 12* at M4n4→M5n5 and +114 ± 20* at M5n5→M6n6), so the
    level-off is the first one, not a lasting plateau. Paris et al.'s wording,
    'began to flatten out' (trout) and 'began to plateau' (earthworm), refers
    to where the levelling starts. They found that higher M adds 'a small
    number of loci that contained many SNPs' at each increment: 'With high
    coverage, these are likely to be true loci', and with low coverage 'more
    likely to be erroneously assembled loci'. Mean ustacks coverage (after
    merging alleles) here is 41.4x–46.0x; Paris et al.'s datasets averaged 21x
    (earthworm), 28x (trout) and 49x (penguin) after merging alleles at m3.
  - SNPs in r80 loci (Paris Fig. 3c) go from 25,767 at M1n1 to 24,138 at M6n6
    (-6.3%). Paris et al. saw a steep rise in SNPs with M in trout and
    earthworm and 'a much smaller increase' in penguin. In trout it 'reflects
    the higher value of M required'; in earthworm they put the continued rise
    in r80 polymorphism down to 'a small number of loci with a high-density of
    SNPs'; in penguin 'the majority of polymorphism and SNPs were already
    captured' at M1.
  - Over-merging signs Paris et al. name, as shares of polymorphic r80 loci
    from the pick upwards: loci with ≥5 SNPs in the forward read go from 0.35%
    at M2n2 to 2.25% at M6n6 (more than two standard errors higher); loci with
    a site where at least 70% of all samples are heterozygous go from 1.50% at
    M2n2 to 1.70% at M6n6 (within two standard errors). With paired-end data
    only the forward read is assembled under M and n, so only its SNPs are
    counted; ≥5 there is the same density as ≥10 in a 282 bp locus. Paris et
    al. note that SNP-dense loci are likely real at high coverage and likely
    misassembled at low coverage. Over-merged paralogs 'will appear as
    heterozygous in a large majority of individuals'; Paris et al. filter them
    after assembly with populations --max_obs_het, and their Table 2 suggests
    a lower M for a repetitive or polyploid genome. In Stacks 2, --max-obs-het
    removes SNPs, not loci; to drop whole loci, blacklist them. The thresholds
    used here are this script's, not Paris et al.'s.
  - Paris et al. suggest testing M1 to M8 (Table 2); this series stops at
    M6n6.

Recommendation following Paris et al. (2017)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  m3 M2 n3
  - m: 3 (the only value tested). Paris et al. suggest testing m3 to m7 and
    chose m3 for all three of their datasets.
  - M: 2, from the M = n series (m3 fixed), because the r80 maximum (M1n1)
    lies below where polymorphic loci within individuals (heterozygous loci
    per individual) first level off (M2n2). Paris et al. never chose M below
    that point (trout M5, earthworm M3, penguin M2), so the higher of the two
    is taken. None of their datasets had the r80 maximum below it, so this
    applies their judgement to a case they did not meet; taken alone, their
    r80 rule gives M1n1.
  - That series changes M and n together. Paris et al. varied M with n fixed
    (n0 in their primary runs), so the r80 counts here reflect n as well as M.
  - n: 3 (Paris et al.'s n rule over n1, n2, n3)

Paris et al. found that fixed SNPs rose with n in all three datasets without
levelling off: 'This alone, however, does not provide enough information to
choose a value for n.' The rise was gentlest in penguin, which has little
divergence between colonies, and steepest in earthworm, which has highly
divergent cryptic lineages. At the n they chose, 15% (trout, n4), 13%
(penguin, n2) and 21% (earthworm, n3) of catalog SNPs were fixed.
